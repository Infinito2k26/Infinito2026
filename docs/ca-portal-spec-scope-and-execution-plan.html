Part One — The CA Portal, Explained

1. What Is the CA Portal?

A Campus Ambassador (CA) is a student at another college who volunteers to promote Infinito 2K26 to their own campus and bring in registrations. The CA Portal is the part of our website built specifically for these volunteers and for the team members who manage them.

It is one focused feature inside the much larger Infinito 2K26 platform. The rest of the platform — event registration, payment, QR check-in on fest day, schedules — is a separate, larger body of work that has not been built yet and is not part of this document.

2. Who Uses It

RoleWho they are
Campus AmbassadorA student volunteer promoting Infinito at their own college.
Admin / Event ManagerAn Infinito team member who approves ambassadors, sets up promotional tasks, and reviews what ambassadors submit.

3. What a Campus Ambassador Can Do

  1. Create an account and log in.
  2. Once approved into the CA role, get a personal referral code and a shareable link (for example, infinito.iitp.ac.in/register?ref=CA-AMU-042).
  3. See a personal dashboard: how many people have clicked their link, how many points they've earned, and where they rank.
  4. See a list of promotional tasks the Infinito team has set up (for example, "post this reel," "share this poster on your college group") and submit proof of completion — either a link or an uploaded photo/screenshot.
  5. See themselves ranked against every other Campus Ambassador on a public leaderboard.

4. What an Event Manager (Admin) Can Do

  1. Approve a registered user into the Campus Ambassador role.
  2. Create promotional tasks — either general tasks the team sets, or tasks tied to a sponsoring brand.
  3. Review what each ambassador submits as proof of completing a task.
  4. Approve or reject each submission, and award (or adjust) the points for it.
  5. View the same leaderboard everyone else sees.

5. How It Works, Step by Step

A new ambassador's journey looks like this: they sign up on the site → an admin approves them into the Campus Ambassador role → they get their personal referral link → they share it on their campus → every click on that link is counted instantly → the Infinito team posts a task → the ambassador completes it and submits proof → an admin reviews and approves it → points are added to their score → their rank on the public leaderboard updates.

One important limitation to understand up front: at launch, we can count how many people clicked an ambassador's link, but we cannot yet automatically confirm how many of those clicks turned into an actual, paid registration. That's because the registration-and-payment part of the platform doesn't exist yet — it's a separate, larger project. Click counts are real and live; registration attribution is not available until that later system is built. This is a known, accepted gap for launch week, not an oversight.

7. What We're Deliberately Leaving Out For Now

These are conscious decisions to keep launch week realistic — not things we forgot. Each one can be added later without reworking anything we build now.

  • Automatic checking of YouTube/Twitter view counts. An admin will look at a submitted link or screenshot and approve it by hand. Same end result for the ambassador — just a manual step for us instead of an automatic one.
  • Signing in with Instagram, LinkedIn, or a college Microsoft account. Only plain email/password sign-in at launch.
  • A separate login area for sponsoring brands. Brands don't get their own accounts — our team manages brand-sponsored tasks on their behalf.
  • Redeeming points for a prize. Points are for ranking on the leaderboard only, for now.
  • Automatically confirming a click turned into a paid registration. As explained in Section 5 — this depends on the registration system, which is a separate, larger project.

10. Things to Keep in Mind (Plain-Language Risk Notes)

  • The site may feel slow on its very first visit of the day. We're launching on free hosting this week (see Part Two, Section 5) to avoid a paid setup we haven't budgeted for yet. Free hosting "falls asleep" after a few minutes of no visitors and takes up to a minute to wake up on the next visit. After that, it's normal speed. This is fine for launch week and gets fixed automatically once we move to paid hosting later — no rebuilding required.
  • Registration numbers tied to a specific ambassador won't be available at launch — only click counts. See Section 5.
  • Task approval is manual at launch. Admins will personally check each submission rather than having the system auto-verify social media stats. This just means a small amount of admin review time per submission.
  • This is a genuinely tight five-day build. The plan is realistic, not padded — if anything unexpected comes up, the first thing to slip would be timeline, not the quality of what ships.

Part Two — Technical Execution Plan

For the engineering team. Precise, task-by-task.

1. System Overview

Monorepo with two applications: apps/api (NestJS backend) and apps/web (Next.js frontend), sharing a PostgreSQL database (via Prisma) and a Redis instance for caching and background job processing (via BullMQ). All new endpoints must follow the existing response-envelope contract and module-boundary rules already documented in CONSTITUTION.md, .claude/reference/architecture.md, and .claude/reference/api.md.

2. Decisions Locked This Week (2026-07-15)

DecisionOutcome

HostingFree-tier stopgap stack (no infra budget sanctioned yet). See Section 5.
Social auto-verificationDeferred to fast-follow. Admins verify proof manually at launch.
Registration-linked attributionClicks-only at launch. The conversion-recording method is built but intentionally left unwired — no Registration module exists yet to call it.

3. Backend Plan — Precise Task List (Owner: Minhaj)

3.1 Endpoints to Build

MethodPathAccessPurpose
PATCH/admin/users/:id/roleAdminPromote a user to Campus Ambassador. The only way a user becomes a CA at launch — no self-service application flow.
POST/ca/onboardCampus AmbassadorBody: assigned college name. Generates a unique referral code, creates the CA profile. Returns 409 if a profile already exists.
GET/ca/meCampus AmbassadorReturns profile, referral code + shareable link, live click count, referral count, total points, and rank.
POST/ca/referral/clickPublicBody: referral code. Validates the code exists (404 if not), then increments a click counter. No database write per click — rate-limited.
POST / GET / PATCH/admin/brandsAdmin / ModeratorCRUD for sponsoring brands (name, logo, contact).
POST / GET / PATCH/admin/ca-tasksAdmin / ModeratorCRUD for promotional tasks. Brand-sourced tasks require a brand; moderator-sourced tasks forbid one.
GET/ca/tasksCampus AmbassadorList active tasks with the caller's own submission status, if any.
POST/ca/tasks/:taskId/submitCampus AmbassadorSubmit proof — a URL or an uploaded file. Re-submitting before verification updates the existing submission rather than creating a duplicate.
GET/admin/ca-tasks/:id/assignmentsAdmin / ModeratorPaginated list of submissions for a task, filterable by status.
PATCH/admin/ca-task-assignments/:id/verifyAdmin / ModeratorApprove or reject a submission, with an optional points override. Must never award points twice for the same submission.
GET/leaderboard/caPublicRanked leaderboard, served from a cache that refreshes every 15 minutes; falls back to a live query if the cache is empty.

3.2 Build Order

  1. Add the required environment variables and dependencies (S3-compatible file storage client).
  2. Minimal admin role-promotion endpoint.
  3. CA onboarding + profile endpoint.
  4. Referral click tracking (rate-limited, no synchronous database write).
  5. The conversion-recording method — built and unit-tested, but intentionally not called from anywhere yet (documented in code with a pointer to this plan, so whoever builds the Registration module later finds the hook immediately).
  6. Brand and task admin CRUD, with the brand/moderator cross-validation rule.
  7. Task list + submission endpoint, including file upload.
  8. (Fast-follow, not launch-blocking) Social media auto-metric fetch.
  9. Admin verification + points award, with strict duplicate-award protection.
  10. Leaderboard computation, caching, and endpoint.
  11. Update .claude/reference/api.md and architecture.md to reflect exactly what shipped.

3.3 Non-Negotiable Acceptance Criteria

  • Referral clicks never cause a synchronous database write.
  • Brand tasks require a brand; moderator tasks forbid one.
  • A submission cannot be verified twice — points cannot double-award.
  • File uploads validate content type and size server-side, not just on the frontend.
  • The leaderboard cache survives a cold start by falling back to a live query.
  • Reference docs (api.md, architecture.md) describe exactly what was built, updated in the same pull request.
  • No cross-module service imports — the CA module talks to Auth only through the authenticated request, never by importing Auth's service directly.

4. Frontend Plan — Precise Task List (Owner: Anjneya, with frontend team support)

Tracked in GitHub issue #25. Branch: feature/ca-frontend, off fresh origin/develop, after the backend endpoints above start landing (API contract is locked above, so this can start in parallel — do not wait for every endpoint to be finished before starting).

4.1 Design & UI Rules — Read Before Building Anything

  • Reuse the existing design system only. Button, Card, Badge, Input, Modal, Spinner, EmptyState, ErrorState, and NotFound components are already built — use them. Do not create new base components for things these already cover.
  • Styling stays in CSS Modules. No Tailwind, no new styling system.
  • Mobile-first is mandatory. Every new page must be checked at a 375px width, not just desktop.
  • Every new page needs a loading state, an empty state, and an error state — verified by actually looking at it in the browser, not assumed from reading the code.
  • No new component libraries or state-management libraries unless a specific, real need comes up while building — do not add anything speculatively.
  • Keyboard accessibility on every button and link — tab order should make sense, nothing should be a click-only trap.

4.3 Also Required

  • A small shared API client that talks to the backend, attaches the login token automatically, and handles an expired-session refresh without the user noticing.
  • Referral-link capture: when someone visits the site with a referral code in the link (e.g. ?ref=CA-AMU-042), the code must be remembered for 30 days and the click recorded immediately — without ever blocking or slowing down the page for the visitor.

4.4 Non-Negotiable Acceptance Criteria

  • Full click-through works end to end: visiting a referral link sets the code, records the click, and the ambassador sees it reflected on their dashboard.
  • Full click-through works end to end: a task submission, once approved by an admin, visibly updates the ambassador's points and leaderboard position.
  • Loading, empty, and error states are verified in-browser for every new page.
  • Every new page is checked at 375px width.
  • No dependency is added beyond what's explicitly needed for this work.

5. Deployment Plan — Free-Tier Launch Stack

No hosting budget is sanctioned yet, so launch runs on free services this week. Moving to the paid, long-term hosting plan later is a configuration change only — no code rewrite.

PieceServiceNote
FrontendVercel (free tier)Already the intended long-term choice.
Backend APIRender or Fly.io (free tier)Background job processing runs inside the same instance — no separate worker service.
DatabaseNeon (free Postgres)Doesn't expire after a trial period, unlike some other free database offers.
Cache / background jobsUpstash (free Redis)Drop-in compatible with what the code already expects.
File storage (task proof uploads)Cloudflare R2 (free tier)Free up to 10GB, no payment method required. Already the intended long-term choice.
DomainDefault Vercel/Render subdomains for nowA custom domain isn't blocking launch — can be added later with zero code change.

Known limitation, flagged deliberately: free backend instances "sleep" after roughly 15 minutes of no traffic, and the first request after that takes 30–50 seconds to respond. Acceptable for launch week; not a long-term solution. Upgrading to an always-on instance later is a billing change only, not a code change. A free uptime monitor should be set up to check on the service periodically so it doesn't stay asleep when someone actually needs it.
